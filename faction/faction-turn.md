# The Faction Turn

> *"The war was decided in a warehouse in Third Ward, by a clerk deciding which requisition to stamp first. History gave the credit to a general."*
> — from the collected letters of a Longhaul cargo master

The faction turn is the organizational heartbeat: the faction earns, pays, and acts as a body, and every character decides whether this stretch of quiet went to the cause or to themselves. It takes twenty to thirty minutes, played together.

**It runs when the GM lets time pass** — after a job, over a journey, at the turn of a season. There is no required cadence. A breathless arc might go three sessions without one; a slow winter might run two back to back. When the story exhales, the machine turns, and what the turn *represents* stretches with it: a turn after a frantic week covers that week; one after a quiet half-year covers the half-year.

Deal the steps out around the table. The player whose Seat matches a step is its natural owner, but anyone can run anything.

## The Three Steps

### 1 · Income

Each holding and enterprise produces per its Gives line. Add the stock to the Ledger, noting where it lands. Seat synergies from doubled Seats land here. While any [hunger condition](faction/resources.md#going-hungry) holds, its income line produces 1 less.

### 2 · Keep

The faction's needs come due — the short list of what existing costs. Two kinds of need sit in the Keep:

- **Consumed needs** are paid and spent each turn: rations eaten, wages drawn, powder burned. Pay them from stock that can reach the need (Ready or Reachable from where it lives), and that stock is gone.
- **Standing needs** must be *present* but are not spent: a garrison that needs 1 Morale to stay loyal, a shrine that needs 1 Unity to stay holy. The stock stays on the Ledger, earmarked to that need — it simply has to keep existing there. A standing need costs nothing per turn, and goes unnoticed until the day that stock is spent, lost, or stolen out from under it. Then the need is suddenly unmet, and hunger begins.

Can't meet a need, or won't? See [Going Hungry](faction/resources.md#going-hungry). Meet every need with stock to spare and the faction is **Flourishing** until next turn — one income line produces 1 extra, or its people defend their places with an edge.

*Income lands first, then the reckoning. The drama of most turns is decided right here — who eats, and what you had to promise away to feed them.*

**Time also moves the map at this step:** Remote stock becomes Reachable, Reachable becomes Ready where the faction means to use it, unless it's [Cut Off](faction/nodes.md#accessibility).

### 3 · Do Something

Each player takes **one move**, and here is the turn's central question made concrete: **a faction action, or a personal one.** Serve the cause, or serve yourself. You get one. The evening is only so long.

Every move is either **immediate** — one roll, one result, done — or a **[project](core/contests.md#project-countdowns)**: a countdown you spend a move to start, and more moves to strike down. Starting a project takes no roll. Striking one does. Only one person may strike a given countdown per turn, though others may spend their move to [aid](core/contests.md#aid) that roll, and you may only have one project of your own running at a time.

**The Head is the exception to one move each:** every Head takes an extra **faction action** on top of their move, and must take it personally.

## Faction Actions

Each faction action is led by one player, who says how their character drives it, and rolled as a normal [contest](core/contests.md) on the faction stat listed. Committed stock is spent win or lose — the pamphlets got printed either way. Against a rival, the GM rolls the rival's stat; against the world, a difficulty.

Each action has a Seat that does it best. That Seat's holder gains a Craft edge when they lead it — or when they aid it.

### Immediate

| Action | Seat | Stat | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Recruit** | Mouth | Ideological | Gain 1 external stock of Zeal or Force — bodies and believers (2 on a Triumph) |
| **Strike** | Fist | Physical | Bring force against a rival's holding, cache, or asset. Big strikes deserve a full [faction conflict](combat/faction.md) instead |
| **Spy** | Eye | Mental | Gain 1 external Mental stock, or ask one true question about a rival's strength, position, or intent |
| **Ferry** | Heel | Physical | Move stock and assets across the map: improve the [accessibility](faction/nodes.md#accessibility) of up to two entries by one step, or run one shipment through danger |
| **Strategize** | Mind | Mental | The faction banks a primed edge, held for a coming faction conflict |
| **Fortify** | Arm | Physical | Clear a physical condition from a holding or asset — repair the walls, patch the hull, put out the fire |
| **Quell** | Heart | Emotional | Clear an emotional or morale condition from the faction's own people |
| **Trade** | Spine | Emotional | Swap stock 1:1 with any faction at Friendly or better |

### Projects

| Action | Seat | Stat | Length | What it builds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Campaign** | Mouth | Emotional | 4 · 6 | Win a district's loyalty or forge a standing relationship (4); recruit a named NPC of real standing (6) |
| **Train** | Fist | Physical | 4 | Raise an asset's rating by 1, or drill the faction for a specific coming conflict (everyone involved starts it with a primed edge) |
| **Compromise** | Eye | Mental | 4 · 6 | Pin a lasting condition on a rival (4); establish a standing information hub inside their walls (6) |
| **Network** | Heel | Physical | 4 · 6 | Open a route, relay, or transit line — through friendly ground (4) or hostile (6). Stock moves a step better along it, permanently |
| **Plan** | Mind | Mental | 4 · 6 · 8 | Gather what a new asset needs (4); earn a new [faction gift](characters/traits.md#faction-gifts) (6); raise a faction stat by 1, max 5 (8) |
| **Develop** | Arm | Physical | 4 · 6 · 8 | Raise a building or fortification (4); establish a new [holding](faction/nodes.md) (6); reshape the ground itself — drain the marsh, wall the pass, terraform the belt (8) |
| **Tend** | Heart | Ideological | 4 | Restore 1 Cohesion |
| **Supply** | Spine | Mental | 6 | Open a new source of internal stock — a farm, a foundry, a school, a shrine. It becomes a permanent income line |

## Personal Actions

The same move, spent on yourself. Personal projects roll whichever stat fits what you're building — the GM approves it when the countdown starts, and it's locked from there.

### Immediate

| Action | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Prepare** | Bank a primed edge for a coming job — a scouted route, a cached tool, a rehearsed lie. You may bank it for an ally instead of yourself |
| **Recover** | Clear your lasting conditions; put the last job's weight down |

### Projects

| Action | Stat | Length | What it earns |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Improve** | the stat being raised | 8 | +1 to one stat, max 5 |
| **Strengthen** | fits the gift | 4 | A new [gift](characters/traits.md#gifts) |
| **Specialize** | fits the feature | 4 | A new feature from your own Seat's list |
| **Diversify** | fits the feature | 6 | A feature from another Seat's list — the narrative has to show you learning it from someone |
| **Acquire** | fits the work | 4 | A [Personal Resource](core/contests.md#the-six-edges) — a signature weapon, a strange instrument, a contraption nobody else owns |

## Inadequate Leadership

Faction actions roll the faction's stat, not yours. But the faction only moves as well as the person pushing it, and some people have no business pushing.

> **If you lead or aid a faction action rolled on a stat you personally rate 1 or 2, you take a snag: *Inadequate Leadership*.**

The faction's Mental might be 4, but if the Fist with Mental 2 volunteers to run the research project, the work suffers for it. This is not a prohibition — anyone may attempt anything — it's a reason the table sorts itself out, and a reason the Mind's opinion matters when the Mind is the one who should be going.

## What Isn't on These Lists

These twenty-three moves are the well-worn paths, not the fence line. A faction can attempt anything its people could plausibly attempt: shedding a temporary flaw, buying off a magistrate, staging a funeral that has to go exactly right. If a player describes something sensible that isn't listed, the GM sets a stat, decides whether it's immediate or a project, picks a length, and it joins the list for that campaign.

The GM may also make an action unavailable, or make one longer or shorter than listed, when the situation calls for it. A faction with no printing press does not get to Campaign across a continent in four segments.

And nothing here is the only route to what it earns. See [a countdown is effort, not a toll gate](core/contests.md#project-countdowns) — good fortune, sharp play, and generous NPCs can hand over the same prizes without a single segment struck.

**Big swings deserve screen time.** The GM should regularly answer an ambitious faction action with *"that's a mission — do you want to play it?"* The faction turn is for the machine; the table is for the story.

## Then the Rivals Move

After the players' turn, each [rival faction](faction/rivals.md) takes one action toward its current goal, and the GM ticks whatever the world advanced on its own. Do it visibly when the narrative allows — rumors, headlines, troop movements.

For tables of six or more players, give major rivals two actions. Eight player moves against one rival action is not a rivalry; it's target practice.
